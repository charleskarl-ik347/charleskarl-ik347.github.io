About Me

Hey there! I'm Charles Yen, a high school student interested in physics, scientific computing, and transportation systems. My curiosity began with railway maps and gradually expanded into programming, mathematics, and physics. Today, I explore these interests through computational modeling, experimental investigations, and collaborative research.

I'm particularly interested in quantum optics, scientific computing, and how mathematical models describe physical phenomena. Outside research, I enjoy designing fictional railway networks, experimenting with programming projects, and exploring questions that occasionally lead me down unexpected rabbit holes. This website documents what I've built, investigated, and learned along the way.

Why Physics?

Physics gives me a way to investigate how the world works, from the behavior of individual particles to the motion of objects we encounter every day. What fascinates me is the relationship between abstract mathematics and physical reality.

A mathematical model may produce a convincing result, but understanding its assumptions and limitations is just as important as obtaining the solution. Through experimentation and computational modeling, I've learned to ask not only whether a calculation is correct, but whether it accurately represents the physical system I'm trying to understand.

Me and Trains

My fascination with railway systems began in childhood, when I spent hours examining transit maps and imagining how different routes connected cities. Eventually, I began designing fictional railway networks of my own.

What started as simple lines on a map gradually developed into a long-running project involving routes, station layouts, service patterns, and timetables. Through simulation environments such as OpenTTD, I also explored signaling, track capacity, and network operations.

These projects taught me how quickly a seemingly simple design can become complicated. Although many of my earlier plans still need substantial revision, I continue to enjoy investigating how transportation networks function and how their constraints influence railway planning.
